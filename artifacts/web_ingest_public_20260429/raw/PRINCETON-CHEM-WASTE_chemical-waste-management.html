Chemical Waste Management

  • Accumulation
  • Storage
  • Disposal

Accumulation

Place chemical waste in sealable containers compatible with the waste. Care should be taken to avoid overfilling waste containers. Filled waste containers should have adequate headspace to allow for expansion of the contents and to minimize spillage when container is being handled or transported.

EHS provides assorted waste containers on the EHS Safety Supply Store under Chemical Waste Containers and Accessories.

Keep containers closed except when filling. Do not leave a hazardous waste container with an open funnel in it. If an ECO Funnel is used, lid to the funnel must be fully closed with latch engaged when not actively making additions to the waste container.

Label waste containers as soon as they contain waste. Containers holding hazardous waste must be labeled with the words HAZARDOUS WASTE and with the names of the principal chemical constituents and the approximate percentage.

  • EHS recommends the use of our formatted waste labels. Contact EHS for waste labels.
  • Use IUPAC or full chemical names (in English); no abbreviations, symbols, structural diagrams or product trade names.
  • Labels must be legible should include contact information of the generator.  

Waste label should be replaced if the label has become damaged or when the contents of the waste container no longer match the description provided on the label.

Waste labels may be requested from the EHS Safety Supply Store.  Blank Waste Label stickers are available under “Labels and Stickers.”  Sheets of prepared waste labels for recurrent waste streams may be requested at the bottom of the Safety Supply Store webform.

Fillable PDF templates are available for download on this website.  Labels are formatted for use with Avery 5164 printable mailing labels.

  • Blank – simple non-fillable pdf template
  • Single Line Labels – for logging waste with up to 5 chemical constituents.
  • Multiline Labels – for logging waste with more than 5 chemical constituents.  Separate each chemical name and percentage with a semi-colon.
  • Individual – each of the six waste labels on the sheet may be filled independently
  • Duplicate – all labels will be autofilled from the first label.

Storage

Containers of hazardous waste may be stored in an area of a laboratory or facilities operation near the point of generation. This area must be controlled by the principal investigator or workers generating the waste. NJDEP and EPA  require the following:​

  • Be sure that containers do not leak. Use secondary containment near sinks and floor drains.
  • No more than one quart of an acutely hazardous waste (P-listed wastes) or 55 gallons of other hazardous wastes may be stored in the accumulation area. If this threshold quantity is reached, the worker must transfer the waste to a 90-day storage area. Contact EHS immediately.
  • Segregate containers with incompatible wastes.
  • Must be capped at all times, unless actively using container.

Disposal 

Disposal Policy

Responsibility for classifying waste as hazardous waste rests with the generator. The federally established scheme for this determination is described below in Hazardous Waste Determination. EHS can assist in making the determination.

The costs of routine chemical waste disposal are not charged back to generators. The costs associated with characterizing mismanaged materials and for disposing of improperly stored materials (e.g., outdated peroxidizable ethers, dry picric acid, deteriorated or unlabeled containers, unknowns) are borne by the generator’s department by EHS discretion.

See Waste Removal and Pick-up for more information.
